The zodiac follows the annual path of the Sun. The lunar mansions follow the Moon.

Across several astronomical and astrological traditions, the Moon's monthly circuit was divided into stations marked by stars or by equal portions of the ecliptic. In Arabic these stations are called *manazil al-qamar*, the mansions of the Moon. Lists of twenty-eight mansions entered medieval Latin astrology and later Western magical texts, where each mansion acquired images, petitions, materials, and electional uses.

Modern guides often present one neat table: mansion number, zodiacal degrees, angel, image, and purpose. The historical reality is less tidy. Indian nakshatras, Chinese lunar lodges, Arabic star-based mansions, equal zodiacal divisions, Picatrix image magic, and later European occult correspondences are related through the Moon's motion, but they are not one interchangeable system.

A responsible practice begins by choosing a lineage and calculation method. Only then can a mansion become more than an exotic label.

## Why twenty-eight mansions?

The Moon returns to approximately the same position among the stars in about 27.3 days, its sidereal month. A twenty-eightfold division creates a practical nightly sequence close to that cycle. Historically, the stations were associated with recognizable stars or asterisms along the Moon's route.

The solar zodiac divides the ecliptic into twelve signs of thirty degrees. A common later mansion scheme divides the full 360 degrees into twenty-eight equal parts of 12 degrees 51 minutes 26 seconds each. These equal divisions are mathematically clean, but they are not identical to every older star-based boundary.

That distinction explains why two calculators may assign the same Moon to different mansions. One may use tropical longitude and equal divisions beginning at 0 Aries. Another may use a sidereal zodiac. Another may anchor mansions to traditional stars. The disagreement is not always a software error; it may be a difference of system.

## Arabic mansions are not simply zodiac slices

In Arabic astronomical culture, the lunar mansions were connected with stars, calendrical observation, weather lore, navigation, and the Moon's visible progress. The system had practical life beyond natal interpretation.

Al-Biruni's *Book of Instruction in the Elements of the Art of Astrology* records astronomical and astrological knowledge, including the mansions and associated information. His work is valuable because it shows classification inside a broader mathematical discipline rather than presenting the mansions as detached spell prompts.

When texts moved into Latin, names were transliterated in varied forms. The first mansion, associated with the stars of the Ram's head, may appear as al-Sharatain, Alnath, or another spelling depending on source. Similar variation affects the entire sequence.

Name variation should not be "corrected" by inventing one universal spelling. A table should store alternate names and identify the source used.

## Tropical, sidereal, and stellar calculation

Before interpreting a mansion, record four settings:

1. **Zodiac:** tropical or sidereal.
2. **Boundary method:** equal divisions or stellar stations.
3. **Starting point:** the longitude or star that begins mansion one.
4. **Time standard:** exact UTC calculation converted correctly from the local birth or election time.

In a tropical equal-division system beginning at 0 Aries, the first mansion extends from 0 Aries to approximately 12 Aries 51. The second continues to approximately 25 Aries 43, and so on. Precession means these tropical segments no longer remain fixed to the same background stars that inspired the historical Arabic names.

In a sidereal or stellar method, the boundaries remain related to the stars but depend on the chosen ayanamsha or stellar definitions. Neither method becomes historically universal by being called "traditional."

For client work, transparency matters more than pretending the schools agree. State the system on the report and use it consistently.

## The Moon's mansion is a condition of the Moon

In a natal chart, the mansion can add detail to the Moon's condition. It does not replace the Moon's sign, house, phase, sect role, ruler, speed, latitude, or aspects.

Begin with the ordinary hierarchy:

- Is the chart day or night?
- Is the Moon the sect light?
- What sign and whole-sign house contains it?
- What planet rules that sign, and how is the ruler placed?
- Is the Moon waxing or waning?
- From which planet is it separating, and to which is it applying?
- Is it void by the declared rule?

Only then add the mansion as a secondary lunar layer. If its traditional image or action repeats the Moon's natal ruler and house, it may sharpen the reading. If it contradicts the chart's stronger testimonies, it should not dominate.

This prevents a mansion epithet from becoming a new personality sign.

## Mansions in electional astrology

The mansions became particularly important in elections and astrological magic. A mansion could be selected because its inherited descriptions suited building, travel, reconciliation, protection, separation, trade, agriculture, or another undertaking.

Yet the Moon's mansion alone cannot carry an election. The Moon might occupy a mansion considered useful for friendship while applying to an out-of-sect malefic, ruled by a debilitated planet, and placed in a house contrary to the matter. The election would contain a contradiction.

A stronger procedure judges:

1. the Ascendant and its ruler;
2. the house governing the action and its ruler;
3. the Moon's sign, house, phase, and motion;
4. the Moon's applications;
5. the mansion and its source-specific purpose;
6. the condition of the mansion's proposed ruler, if the source assigns one;
7. natal compatibility when the action is personal and important.

The mansion refines the Moon's office. It does not excuse a broken chart.

## Picatrix and the images of the mansions

The Arabic magical compendium known as the *Ghayat al-Hakim*, translated into Latin as *Picatrix*, became a major source for lunar-mansion images in Western astrological magic. It describes figures to be fashioned under particular celestial conditions for particular ends.

These passages are historically important and ethically mixed. Some seek protection, healing, trade, or concord. Others concern destruction, coercion, separation, or control. Materials and procedures can also be unsafe or unacceptable.

Studying the text does not obligate reproduction. A modern practitioner can preserve the contemplative architecture while rejecting coercion and dangerous materia.

For example, an image associated with safe travel can become the center of a travel-planning observance: verify documents, check routes, contact hosts, begin under a sound election, and use the image as a reminder of preparedness. The practical action fulfills the symbolic purpose without claiming invulnerability.

## Agrippa and the Latin inheritance

Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa's *Three Books of Occult Philosophy* helped transmit mansion images and celestial correspondences into later Western occultism. Agrippa compiles from many sources, and his tables acquired enormous authority in modern magical practice.

Compilation creates a common problem. A later reader sees an item in Agrippa and assumes it was universal across Arabic, Greek, Jewish, and Indian astrology. It was not. Agrippa's work belongs to a sixteenth-century Christian and Neoplatonic synthesis.

When a modern table uses an angel name, divine name, image, perfume, or purpose, the source chain should be visible. Is the item from the *Picatrix*, Agrippa, a grimoire, a nineteenth-century order, or a contemporary author? Different provenance may be acceptable, but silent mixing is not.

This source discipline is also essential in [Agrippa's celestial magic](/blog/cornelius-agrippa-celestial-magic-astrology).

## Mansion rulers are not universally fixed

Online lists often assign a planet, angel, or zodiacal ruler to every mansion as though the mapping were ancient and uncontested. Many such lists combine several systems.

The Moon itself governs the cycle in the broadest sense. Individual mansions may receive star, planetary, elemental, or angelic associations depending on the text. A modern system may calculate a mansion by tropical degree but attach a name derived from a sidereal stellar station and an angel from a much later Western table.

That combination can be used as a chosen ritual system, but it must be labeled as synthesis. It should not be advertised as one original Arabic doctrine.

A reliable database should version each correspondence rather than overwrite disagreement:

- `tradition`
- `source_work`
- `edition_or_translation`
- `mansion_number`
- `primary_name`
- `alternate_names`
- `calculation_system`
- `image_description`
- `purpose`
- `prohibited_or_unsafe_elements`
- `editorial_note`

This turns a mysterious table into auditable scholarship.

## Birth time and mansion boundaries

The Moon moves roughly twelve to fifteen degrees per day. It can cross an equal mansion in about a day, sometimes faster or slower. Near a boundary, a small birth-time error may change the mansion.

If the recorded time is uncertain, calculate the Moon at the earliest and latest plausible times. If it remains in one mansion, the assignment is stable. If it crosses, the report should show both possibilities or omit mansion-specific claims.

The same caution applies when the birth date is known but time is unknown. The Moon may change sign, mansion, or applying aspect during the day. A trustworthy report names the uncertainty.

Precision is not decoration. It determines whether the symbol belongs to the chart.

## A non-coercive mansion practice

The following form keeps the Moon, election, and practical action together.

1. Choose a purpose concerning your own conduct: study, repair, preparation, release, hospitality, or disciplined rest.
2. Find a mansion whose source-specific purpose is compatible.
3. Calculate the Moon with a declared zodiac and boundary system.
4. Reject the time if the Ascendant ruler or Moon is severely impeded for the matter.
5. Use a safe image copied from or inspired by the historical description without toxic or coercive elements.
6. State a petition focused on your capacity and action.
7. Complete a concrete task aligned with the purpose.
8. Record the time, chart, source, action, and result.

If the purpose involves another person, preserve consent. A mansion for reconciliation can support an honest apology and invitation to dialogue. It cannot guarantee the other person will return.

## The mansion as a monthly study rhythm

The mansions can also be studied without magic. Follow the Moon for twenty-eight stations and keep a simple record:

- mansion and calculation system;
- Moon sign and house relative to your natal chart;
- lunar phase;
- applying aspect;
- dominant activity and mood;
- notable external events;
- what did not fit the inherited description.

After several cycles, compare rather than proclaim. You may discover that transits to natal rulers explain more than the mansion. You may also notice that certain mansion themes become clearer when the Moon activates a relevant natal house.

This is useful knowledge. A technique earns confidence through repeated, recorded discrimination, not through one dramatic coincidence.

## Mansions in a personal astrology product

A mansion feature can add value to a lunar calendar or Hermetic report, but only if the product avoids false precision.

The report should display the chosen system, exact Moon longitude, mansion boundaries, source tradition, and confidence level. It should distinguish historical description from modern practical adaptation. It should never invent angel names or psalm correspondences through generative prose.

For a monthly almanac, the mansion is best used selectively. Include it when a New Moon, Full Moon, or important election activates the client's natal chart. Repeating twenty-eight generic mansion paragraphs every month would add bulk without personalization.

## Common errors

The first error is mixing tropical boundaries with sidereal claims without disclosure.

The second is treating Arabic, Indian, Chinese, and Western mansion systems as identical.

The third is reading the mansion before the Moon's sign, house, ruler, phase, and applications.

The fourth is choosing an election solely from a mansion keyword.

The fifth is copying dangerous or coercive recipes because they are old.

The sixth is presenting later angelic correspondences as ancient Arabic consensus.

The seventh is ignoring birth-time uncertainty near a boundary.

The eighth is interpreting every event after the fact while recording no misses.

## A moving house for the Moon

The lunar mansions preserve a way of watching the sky night by night. Their names remember stars, seasons, roads, weather, images, and inherited acts. Their later magical forms show how astrologers tried to coordinate human beginnings with the Moon's changing station.

Their richness requires boundaries. Choose one calculation. Name one source. Judge the Moon's full condition. Reject coercion. Keep the natal chart above the decorative correspondence. Record what actually happens.

Then the mansion becomes what its name suggests: a temporary place through which the Moon passes. It is not a permanent identity. It is a room in the lunar journey, useful because we know where its doors came from.
