When a planet approaches the Sun, traditional astrologers do not read the conjunction as an ordinary blending of two psychological traits. The Sun is the source of daylight. A planet too close to it disappears from the visible sky, and that loss of visibility becomes part of the judgment.

Three terms organize the subject: **under the beams**, **combustion**, and **cazimi**. They describe different degrees of solar proximity, not three synonyms for the same condition. A planet under the beams is obscured. A combust planet is more deeply overwhelmed by the Sun. A planet in the heart of the Sun, or cazimi, occupies a very narrow zone that many medieval astrologers treated as exceptional strength.

These conditions are often flattened online into a frightening slogan: the Sun “burns up” the planet. That image is memorable but incomplete. Solar proximity can describe concealment, dependence on authority, difficulty acting independently, intense concentration, or a function absorbed into questions of identity and visibility. The result depends on the planet, its houses, dignity, sect, motion, phase, and the rest of the chart.

## Visibility is the foundation

Traditional astrology developed under a sky that astrologers actually watched. Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn can be seen with the naked eye, but not while the Sun's light hides them. Their first and last appearances around the Sun were observable events. Astrological language about being hidden, emerging, setting, or rising belongs to this astronomy of visibility.

A planet disappearing into the Sun's rays loses the ability to testify openly. That does not mean it ceases to exist. It means its action becomes difficult to distinguish from the solar agenda. The planet may operate behind a leader, institution, father figure, vocation, public role, or consuming need for recognition. Sometimes its topics are private until a later period brings them into view.

This is why a conjunction by longitude is not enough. Two charts can both contain Mercury conjunct the Sun while describing very different Mercurial conditions. In one, Mercury may be cazimi, direct, swift, dignified, and angular. In another, it may be combust, retrograde, slow, cadent, and responsible for difficult houses. The shared conjunction does not erase the hierarchy of condition.

## Under the beams

“Under the beams” is the broadest condition. Medieval authorities commonly use a range around fifteen degrees from the Sun, although exact limits vary by author. Within that zone, a planet is concealed by sunlight but not necessarily in the most intense state of combustion.

Under the beams can signify something real but not fully visible. A Venus under the beams may describe private agreements, values shaped by a dominant solar purpose, or relational matters that take time to become explicit. A Jupiter under the beams may indicate counsel operating behind power, protected knowledge, or growth that remains connected to the approval of a central authority.

The symbolism can be difficult when the planet must act publicly. If the ruler of the tenth house is under the beams, recognition may arrive indirectly, through another person's name, or after a period of hidden labor. If the ruler of the second is concealed, resources may be poorly accounted for or tied to solar topics. Yet concealment can be appropriate for confidential research, spiritual retreat, private planning, or work whose value depends on discretion.

House rulership therefore matters. The condition belongs not only to the planet's generic meanings but also to every house it governs. This is one reason a serious reading begins with [houses and their rulers](/blog/houses-in-astrology), not a list of planetary keywords.

## Combustion

Combustion is the more severe state within the beams. A widely used medieval boundary is approximately eight degrees and thirty minutes from the Sun, though traditional texts and later schools do not all use precisely the same limit. The important point is conceptual: the planet is close enough that solar power dominates its capacity to appear and act independently.

Combustion may describe pressure, absorption, fear of exposure, loss of autonomy, or action carried out in the name of something larger and brighter. The combust planet can become intensely identified with the Sun's concerns. A combust Mars may struggle to separate decisive action from pride, authority, or the need to prove vitality. A combust Venus may find that desire, reciprocity, and value are drawn into questions of identity or recognition.

This is not a moral judgment. The person is not defective, and the planet is not “destroyed.” A combust significator may become extraordinarily concentrated. The difficulty is proportion: can the planetary function speak in its own language, or must it always serve the solar center?

The answer changes with [essential dignity](/blog/essential-dignity-astrology). A dignified combust planet may possess resources, knowledge, or authority even while operating under solar dominance. A planet with little essential dignity may have fewer stable tools. Angularity can make the condition louder and more consequential; cadency can make it quieter or harder to mobilize. Sect changes how naturally a planet participates in the chart's day or night environment.

## Cazimi: in the heart of the Sun

Cazimi comes from an Arabic expression meaning “as if in the heart.” Many medieval astrologers used a distance of roughly seventeen minutes of arc from the Sun. That is an extremely narrow range: less than one third of a degree. A planet at 10 degrees 10 minutes Leo with the Sun at 10 degrees Leo would qualify by that rule; a planet one full degree away would not.

The traditional image reverses the usual problem. Instead of being scorched outside the throne room, the planet is seated with the king. Cazimi can signify authorization, protection, precision, access to power, or an unusual concentration of the planet's capacity.

That does not make every cazimi planet automatically fortunate in every topic. The planet still rules particular houses, occupies a house, has a sign condition, belongs or does not belong to the sect, and forms other relationships. If it rules a difficult topic, its power may make that topic more capable of acting rather than simply pleasant. Strength and beneficence are not identical.

Modern software also requires care. Some programs display rounded degrees, making a planet appear exact when its true separation exceeds the cazimi boundary. Always inspect degrees and minutes, and know whether the program measures longitude or visual contact.

## The planet matters

Mercury and Venus never travel far from the Sun, so solar proximity is part of their natural cycles. Treating every combust Mercury or Venus as a rare catastrophe would make an enormous number of charts unreasonably dire.

Mercury near the Sun may describe a mind intensely linked with authorship, purpose, speech, administration, or identity. The question is whether Mercury can revise, negotiate, and distinguish facts without being recruited immediately into the solar position. Its speed, retrogradation, station proximity, sign, house, and whether it is emerging before or after the Sun all matter.

Venus near the Sun links value, attraction, aesthetics, agreement, and relationship with solar questions. At its best, this can produce artistic coherence or devotion to a craft. Under pressure, it can make reciprocity difficult to separate from approval. Venus's phase as morning or evening star adds another layer that a simple conjunction cannot capture.

Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn spend much longer away from the Sun. Their conjunctions occur within broader synodic cycles. A superior planet entering combustion may signify a temporary period of invisibility before a new cycle of expression. In natal work, the condition is lifelong, but transits and annual techniques can describe when the hidden planet becomes especially active.

## Applying, separating, and moving through the Sun

Direction matters. A planet applying to combustion is entering deeper concealment. One separating from combustion is moving away from the Sun and toward recovery of visibility. The natal chart is a still image of a moving sky, so this phase gives the conjunction a narrative.

An applying planet can describe unfinished absorption: the solar center is gathering the planet. A separating planet has already passed through the encounter. Its topics may carry the memory of dependence or invisibility while possessing a stronger orientation toward emergence.

Retrogradation can complicate the sequence. Mercury or Venus may approach, separate, station, and return. A correct judgment needs daily motion rather than a guess based on zodiacal order. The same principle applies to every [applying and separating aspect](/blog/applying-separating-aspects-astrology): the faster body's actual motion determines whether perfection is approaching.

## Combust house rulers

Suppose the ruler of the seventh house is combust. It would be careless to conclude that relationships are doomed. Better questions include:

- Is the ruler dignified or received by another planet?
- Is it applying to or separating from the Sun?
- Which house contains the conjunction?
- What houses does the Sun rule?
- Do Venus and the Moon repeat the same testimony?
- Is the planet angular, succedent, or cadent?
- Does the condition describe privacy, unequal visibility, or difficulty negotiating independently?

The same method applies to career, money, family, and vocation. A combust ruler of the tenth may show work close to leadership or an institution, authorship that eclipses collaboration, or professional skill that remains hidden behind a public figure. The rest of the chart decides which interpretation has support.

Repeated testimony is essential. One solar condition should not become the entire biography.

## Natal condition and transit timing

A natal combust planet describes an enduring architecture. A transiting combustion is temporary and occurs in every synodic cycle. When the transiting Sun approaches natal or transiting Mercury, for example, the appropriate response is not panic. It may be a period when information is centralized, visibility narrows, or a plan needs privacy before it can be announced.

Timing becomes more personal when the planet is the annual time lord, ruler of an activated house, or a dominant natal significator. A [solar return](/blog/solar-returns-read-year-with-natal-chart) that repeats natal combustion deserves more attention than a generic daily transit. Even then, astrology describes emphasis and conditions, not an unavoidable event.

## Common interpretive mistakes

The first mistake is using one universal orb without naming the tradition. Traditional authors differ, and contemporary astrologers sometimes adopt stricter or wider limits. State the rule before applying it.

The second is confusing strength with goodness. Cazimi can strengthen a planet, but a strong planet may vigorously carry complicated houses. Combustion can weaken independent action, but hidden or concentrated action is not always harmful.

The third is ignoring visibility and treating the conjunction as a modern personality blend. Solar proximity is astronomical symbolism before it is psychological symbolism.

The fourth is neglecting phase. Applying to the Sun, separating from it, retrograding back, or emerging into visibility are different conditions.

The fifth is using frightening language. “Burned up,” “ruined,” and “destroyed” may imitate old metaphors without doing old judgment. Traditional astrology weighs multiple testimonies. Fatalism is not rigor.

## A practical method

When you find a planet close to the Sun, work in this order:

1. Calculate the exact separation in degrees and minutes.
2. Declare the thresholds you are using for beams, combustion, and cazimi.
3. Determine whether the planet is applying or separating.
4. Record direct or retrograde motion, daily speed, and station proximity.
5. Judge sign dignity, sect, house position, and angularity.
6. Identify every house the planet rules.
7. Look for reception and close supporting aspects.
8. Ask whether concealment is harmful, protective, concentrated, or appropriate to the topic.
9. Confirm the conclusion through repeated testimony elsewhere in the chart.

This sequence keeps a dramatic symbol in proportion. It also shows why a chart calculator cannot finish the interpretation. The software can measure distance. Judgment must decide what that distance means in this particular architecture.

## The deeper solar question

Every close solar conjunction asks how one planetary function relates to the organizing center of life. Can Mercury question the identity it helps articulate? Can Venus value something that receives no applause? Can Mars act without turning every conflict into a test of pride? Can Jupiter guide without borrowing authority? Can Saturn establish limits without becoming only the servant of a central ambition?

Combustion does not answer these questions with a sentence. It makes them structurally important. Under the beams describes what is difficult to see. Cazimi describes what has unusual access to the center. The whole chart shows whether that access becomes wisdom, dependency, craft, secrecy, pressure, or power.

That is the difference between spotting a condition and reading it.
